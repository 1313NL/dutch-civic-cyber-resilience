# Pillar 3: Physical & Infrastructure Resilience (Power Outages & Blackouts)

> **Goal:** Ensure 72-hour household self-reliance during major power grid failures, physical cyber-attacks on infrastructure, or severe weather disruptions in the Netherlands.

---

## 1. Why Physical Resilience Matters for Digital Security

Modern digital infrastructure depends entirely on physical systems: power grids, submarine fiber-optic cables, cellular towers, and automated water pumps. 

When a major blackout or physical disruption occurs:
- Mobile cell towers lose battery backup after 2 to 6 hours.
- Digital payment terminals (iDEAL, PIN cards, contactless) stop functioning immediately.
- Municipal water pumps and home heating systems (such as district heating or heat pumps) fail.

**Civic Principle:** Preparing your household to be self-sufficient for at least **72 hours** relieves pressure on emergency services (**112**) and allows response teams to focus on critical life-saving operations.

---

## 2. The 72-Hour Emergency Kit (Household Essentials)

In the event of an extended power grid failure, every household should have immediate access to core survival essentials without relying on open grocery stores or digital deliveries.


+--------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                   72-Hour Survival Essentials                      |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Water        | 3 Liters per person / per day (Drinking & Hygiene)  |
| Food         | Non-perishable, ready-to-eat (No cooking required)  |
| Light        | LED flashlights & headlamps (Avoid open candles)    |
| Power        | Pre-charged power banks (10,000–20,000 mAh)          |
| Cash         | Physical cash in small notes (€5, €10, €20)         |
| Information  | Battery or hand-crank emergency radio (FM/AM)       |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------+

### 1. Water & Food Supplies
- **Water Standard:** Store a minimum of **3 liters of drinking water per person per day** (9 liters per person for a 3-day outage). Do not forget pets.
- **Food Standard:** Stock non-perishable foods that do not require refrigeration or boiling water:
  - Canned beans, vegetables, fish (tuna/sardines), and ready-to-eat soups.
  - Energy bars, nuts, dried fruit, and peanut butter.
  - A manual, non-electric hand can opener.

### 2. Light & Off-Grid Power
- **Flashlights & Headlamps:** Keep LED flashlights or headlamps in easily accessible locations. Avoid using open candles, which pose a severe fire hazard during widespread emergencies.
- **Power Banks:** Keep pre-charged USB power banks (at least 10,000 mAh capacity) reserved exclusively for phone charging and emergency lighting.

---

## 3. Communication Preparedness: Emergency Radios & Offline Contacts

During a prolonged grid outage, local cellular towers and home Wi-Fi routers will go offline. You cannot rely on smartphone apps or news websites for official safety updates.

### Emergency AM/FM/DAB+ Radios
- **Equipment:** Keep a battery-powered or hand-crank emergency radio stored at home with fresh spare batteries.
- **National Emergency Broadcast (Calamiteitzender):** In the Netherlands, regional public broadcasters (such as *RTV Noord*, *Omroep West*, *RTV Rijnmond*, etc.) serve as official emergency channels during major crises.
- **Tuning In:** Tune your emergency radio to local public stations or national frequency **NPO Radio 1** to receive official instructions from safety regions (*Veiligheidsregio's*).

### Physical Emergency Contact Directory
When smartphone batteries die or cloud contacts are unreachable, paper documentation is crucial:
- Maintain a printed, laminated list of essential contact numbers:
  - Immediate family members and local neighbors.
  - Non-emergency police line (**0900-8844**).
  - Local family doctor (huisarts) and local pharmacy.
  - Designated physical meeting location for your family if mobile networks fail completely.

---

## 4. Financial Resilience: Small Cash Reserves

During a power blackout, card readers, ATM machines, online banking apps, and supermarket checkout systems shut down completely.

### The Cash Reserve Protocol
- **Maintain Physical Cash:** Keep a baseline supply of **physical cash** stored safely in your home.
- **Small Denominations:** Store cash in small notes (**€5, €10, and €20 bills**) along with small coins. During a power outage, local shopkeepers and markets operating manually will not have change for €50 or €100 bills.
- **Rule of Thumb:** Keep enough cash to purchase 3 to 5 days of basic food, fuel, or medicine at local cash-only markets.

---

## 5. Home Safety & Heating During Outages

### Winter Preparedness (Cold Snap Grid Failure)
- **Conserve Body Heat:** Gather family members into one small room in the house. Close doors, draw curtains, and layer clothing using thermal wear, blankets, and sleeping bags.
- **Ventilation Safety:** **Never** use outdoor camping stoves, gas heaters, or charcoal grills indoors to heat your home. Doing so creates an invisible, deadly risk of **Carbon Monoxide (CO) poisoning**. Ensure you have a battery-operated CO detector installed in your living space.

---

## 6. Summary Checklist: 72-Hour Physical Kit

- [ ] Store 9 liters of bottled drinking water per person.
- [ ] Stock non-perishable food for 3 days and include a manual can opener.
- [ ] Secure a battery-powered or hand-crank emergency radio.
- [ ] Print a physical paper list of essential emergency contacts and meeting places.
- [ ] Keep €100–€200 in small cash notes (€5, €10, €20) at home.
- [ ] Fully charge two high-capacity power banks and store them in a cool place.
- [ ] Verify battery-operated flashlights and carbon monoxide (CO) detectors are working.
