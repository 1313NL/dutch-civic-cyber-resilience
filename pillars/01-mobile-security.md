# Pillar 1: Mobile Security & Advanced Spyware Mitigations

> **Goal:** Protect your smartphone against silent cyber attacks, understand hidden phone threats, and know what to do if wireless signals are disrupted in your area.

---

## 1. What Are "Zero-Click" Cyber Attacks?

### The Threat in Simple Terms
Normally, a phone hack happens when you click a bad link, download a suspicious file, or open a fake email. 

A **"Zero-Click" attack** is different and much more dangerous. The attacker sends a special, hidden message or video file directly to your phone number or chat application (like WhatsApp or iMessage). **You do not need to click anything, open anything, or do anything.** Your phone processes the incoming message automatically in the background, and the spyware silently installs itself.

Once installed, this type of spyware (often called "Pegasus-class" software) can:
- Read your private chat messages and emails.
- Listen to your phone calls and record room conversations using your microphone.
- Access your location history and photos.

---

## 2. Practical Steps to Protect Your Phone

While zero-click attacks are advanced, you do not need to be a technology expert to block or clean most of them. Follow these simple steps:

### Step 1: Turn Your Phone Off and On Every Day
- **Why this works:** Most silent spyware gets temporarily saved in your phone's short-term memory (RAM), not on the permanent hard drive. When you turn your phone completely off and back on, that short-term memory is wiped clean, breaking the attacker's connection.
- **Action:** Set a daily habit—like when you wake up or go to bed—to completely restart your phone.

### Step 2: Turn On Built-In Security Features
Modern phone makers have built strict protection modes designed specifically to stop silent attacks.

#### On iPhone (iOS): Turn on Lockdown Mode
Lockdown Mode turns off high-risk background features that hackers use to break into phones (like complex web features and automatic message previews).
1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Privacy & Security**.
3. Scroll down to the bottom and tap **Lockdown Mode**.
4. Tap **Turn On Lockdown Mode** and restart your phone.

#### On Android: Turn on Auto-Restart and Strict App Checks
1. Open **Settings**.
2. Go to **Device Care** (or **Security & Privacy** depending on your phone model).
3. Turn on **Auto Restart** so your phone reboots itself overnight automatically.
4. Make sure **Install Unknown Apps** is set to **Off** for all applications.

### Step 3: Keep Your Phone Updated Immediately
- System updates contain fixes for known security holes. Whenever your phone asks to update its operating system (iOS or Android), install it right away.

---

## 3. How to Check If Your Phone Is Safe

If you suspect your phone is behaving strangely (draining battery extremely fast, overheating when not in use, or showing unexpected data usage), open-source tools exist to inspect your device.

- **Amnesty International MVT (Mobile Verification Toolkit):** An open-source, non-profit diagnostic tool that scans your phone's backup files for known traces of professional spyware.
- **Civic Rule:** Never pay private companies large sums of money to "clean" your phone. Stick to verified open-source utilities or official manufacturer support channels.

## 4. Signal Jammers & Wireless Safety in the Netherlands

### What Is a Signal Jammer?
A signal jammer is a small electronic box that sends out strong radio noise to block all nearby mobile connections, Wi-Fi signals, and GPS location tracking.

### Why Are They Dangerous?
When someone uses a signal jammer:
- Nearby phones cannot make emergency calls (**112** in the Netherlands and Europe).
- Navigation systems for ambulances, police, and fire trucks stop working properly.
- Home security alarms and smart grid systems lose connectivity.

### Legal Status & Civic Action
- **The Law:** Signal jammers are **100% illegal to own, buy, or use in the Netherlands** and across the European Union.
- **What to do if signals are blocked:** If you notice sudden, total loss of cellular and Wi-Fi networks across multiple devices in your immediate area without an official blackout:
  1. Do not confront suspicious individuals nearby.
  2. Use a landline or step away to an unaffected area to report suspicious interference.
  3. Contact the **Rijksinspectie Digitale Infrastructuur (RDI)**, the Dutch government agency that monitors radio frequencies and stops illegal interference.
