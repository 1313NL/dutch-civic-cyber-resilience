# Contributor Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

In the interest of fostering an open, welcoming, and safe environment, we as contributors and maintainers pledge to make participation in the **Dutch Civil Preparedness & Digital Resilience Framework** a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

---

## 1. Core Civic & Non-Vendor Rules

Because this project exists strictly to serve the public interest and elevate civil preparedness across the Netherlands, all contributors must adhere to three non-negotiable operational rules:

### A. Non-Vendor Neutrality (Strictly Enforcement)
- **No Commercial Pitching:** This repository is strictly non-commercial. Submissions advocating for specific paid software, proprietary vendor tools, or corporate consulting services will be rejected.
- **No Referral or Affiliate Links:** No links containing affiliate tags, referral codes, or tracking tokens are permitted anywhere in discussions, issues, or pull requests.
- **Built-In & Open-Source First:** Guidance must always prioritize free, built-in operating system security features (e.g., iOS Lockdown Mode, Android security settings) or verified open-source, non-profit utilities (e.g., Amnesty International MVT, Have I Been Pwned, Bitwarden).

### B. Plain-Language Standard
- All documentation, checklists, and discussions must remain accessible to the non-technical public. Avoid elitist tech jargon, unnecessary acronyms, or condescending language when reviewing contributions.

### C. No Fear-Mongering or Disinformation
- Threats must be described accurately, calmly, and objectively. Unverified rumors, alarmist conspiracy theories, or political fear-mongering will be removed immediately.

---

## 2. Expected Behavior

We encourage the following positive behaviors across all repository interactions (Issues, Pull Requests, Discussions):

- **Focusing on what is best for the community:** Always prioritize citizen safety and accessibility over personal or organizational interests.
- **Showing empathy and kindness:** Remember that many readers and contributors may be non-technical citizens seeking safety during stressful situations.
- **Being respectful of differing viewpoints:** Welcome constructive criticism and diverse perspectives on language translation and threat mitigations.
- **Gracefully accepting constructive feedback:** Accept edits or suggestions that make documentation clearer or easier to understand.

---

## 3. Unacceptable Behavior

The following behaviors are considered harassment and are strictly prohibited:

- The use of sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or electronic address, without explicit permission (doxxing).
- Spamming issues or pull requests with commercial advertising, vendor self-promotion, or repetitive off-topic posts.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional or civil setting.

---

## 4. Enforcement Responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for clarifying the standards of acceptable behavior and are expected to take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any instances of unacceptable behavior.

Maintainers have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct, or to ban temporarily or permanently any contributor for other behaviors that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

---

## 5. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces (GitHub Issues, Pull Requests, Discussions) and applies when an individual is representing the project in public spaces using an official project account or email address.

---

## 6. Reporting Violations

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior—or attempts to covertly monetize/promote products within the repository—may be reported by opening a confidential **Issue** flagged as `[CONDUCT]` or by contacting the project maintainers directly.

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. All project maintainers are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident.
